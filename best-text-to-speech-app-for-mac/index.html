What is the best text-to-speech app for Mac?

It depends on what you read. Start with macOS Read & Speak for a built-in shortcut. Choose a native app such as LoudScript, Aloud or Sandbook for offline voices and PDFs. Choose Speechify, NaturalReader or ElevenReader when you want a library that follows you to your phone.

Which Mac text-to-speech app should you pick?

  • No install: macOS Read & Speak speaks selected text with Option-Esc.
  • Free native app for selected text, screenshots and PDFs: LoudScript.
  • Free native app for EPUB books on Apple silicon: Sandbook.
  • One-time purchase with M4A export: Aloud, $19 per Mac.
  • EPUB and PDF library with offline voices: Voice Dream Reader, by subscription.
  • A library that syncs with iPhone and Android: Speechify, NaturalReader or ElevenReader.

What should you compare?

  1. Input: selected text, pasted text, screenshots, PDFs or ebooks.
  2. Reading experience: highlighting, controls, window behavior and history.
  3. Privacy: what stays on the Mac and what goes to a server.
  4. Compatibility: macOS version, Intel or Apple silicon, and languages.
  5. Cost: free, one-time purchase or subscription.
  6. Output: live listening or exported audio files.

What is each app best at?

macOS Read & Speak

Apple’s built-in option lives in System Settings under Accessibility. In macOS Tahoe 26 Apple names the pane Read & Speak. Turn on Speak selection, select text and press Option-Esc. The controller can open Accessibility Reader, which reads text in a customizable view. It needs no extra app.

LoudScript

LoudScript is a free native Mac app. It reads selected text from any app, text in a screenshot, and imported PDF pages, with a floating player above other windows. Voices come from Apple, Supertonic, Kokoro, PocketTTS and Phonon, and all of them run on the Mac. It keeps a searchable reading history and exports WAV audio with Apple and Supertonic voices. It does not import EPUB books or web page links.

Speechify

Speechify has a Mac app that reads selected text in any app with Option-A and reads screenshots aloud. Saved content opens in the Web Library. The free plan has 10 voices up to 1.5x speed. Premium adds more voices and languages.

NaturalReader

NaturalReader’s current personal app runs in the browser, on iOS and Android, and as a Chrome extension. Its free plan includes unlimited listening with free voices. Paid plans add MP3 downloads and OCR. A separate desktop app for Mac, sold once from $99.50, includes a Miniboard that reads selected text in other apps.

ElevenReader

ElevenReader from ElevenLabs runs on the web, iPhone, iPad and Android. Its App Store listing covers iPhone and iPad only. It imports PDFs, EPUBs and articles and gives free accounts 10 hours of listening a month.

Voice Dream Reader

Voice Dream Reader has a Mac App Store app with offline voices, PDF and DRM-free EPUB support, and audio file export. The Mac app needs its own subscription, separate from the iOS app, after a 7-day trial.

Aloud

Aloud is a native menu bar app for macOS 14 or later. It reads selected text, PDFs and screenshots, runs Apple and Kokoro voices on the Mac, and exports M4A. It costs $19 once per Mac.

Sandbook

Sandbook is a free reader for iPhone, iPad and Mac with Kokoro voices built in. It reads EPUB, PDF and Word files and exports M4A audiobooks with chapter markers. On Mac it needs Apple silicon and macOS 15.2 or later.

When is LoudScript the right choice?

  • You move between selected text, screenshots, pasted text and PDF pages.
  • You want the current sentence highlighted in the original PDF.
  • You want a floating player while you work in another window.
  • You want reading history and several local voice models.
  • You want a free app that also runs on Intel Macs. Kokoro and PocketTTS need Apple silicon.

When should you choose something else?

  • You only need an occasional shortcut. macOS Read & Speak may be enough.
  • You read EPUB books. Sandbook and Voice Dream Reader import EPUB. LoudScript does not.
  • You need MP3 or M4A files. NaturalReader exports MP3 on paid plans. Aloud and Sandbook export M4A. LoudScript exports WAV only.
  • You want one library across Mac, iPhone and Android. Speechify, NaturalReader and ElevenReader sync across devices.
  • You need studio narration or voice cloning.
  • Your Mac cannot run macOS 15.6 or later. Voice Dream Reader lists macOS 11 and Aloud lists macOS 14.
  • You need the iPhone or iPad edition of LoudScript. Read the platform comparison.

What stays on your Mac with LoudScript?

Apple voices and LoudScript’s downloaded speech models synthesize on your Mac. Screenshot OCR uses Apple’s on-device Vision framework. LoudScript does not send raw speech text, OCR output or source-app identity to product analytics.

Optional AI formatting is the exception. It is off until you turn it on. When it is on, the text being formatted goes through LoudScript’s server to a third-party AI model provider. Read the privacy policy before you enable it.

Try LoudScript on your Mac

LoudScript is a free, signed and notarized download for macOS 15.6 or later. It supports Intel and Apple silicon Macs, although Kokoro and PocketTTS require Apple silicon, and Phonon has been tested only on Apple silicon.
